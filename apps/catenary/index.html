Catenary vs. Parabola

A cable hanging under its own weight follows an exact catenary. Engineers often approximate it with a parabola — the exact shape for a load spread uniformly over the horizontal span rather than along the cable, like a suspension-bridge deck. Drag the sliders to change the span, support height difference, how much slack cable you have, and its mass per length, and watch the two curves agree closely for a tight cable, then diverge as the sag grows.

What's the difference? A chain or cable hanging only under its own weight carries that weight per unit length along the cable — the exact equilibrium shape is a catenary, y(x) = a·cosh((x−x₀)/a). The parabola instead assumes the same total weight is spread evenly over the horizontal projection — the classic suspension-bridge idealisation, and a textbook approximation for shallow cables since ds ≈ dx when the sag is small. The horizontal tension is constant along either curve; tension is largest at whichever support is higher and/or farther from the low point. Both solutions are solved here in closed form (bisection on a single transcendental equation) — no simulation, just statics.
