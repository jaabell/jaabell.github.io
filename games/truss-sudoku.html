      ❓ How to play

      Every truss here is statically determinate: it has exactly enough equations to solve exactly, no more, no less — same spirit as a well-posed sudoku grid.

      Phase 1 — Reactions. Analyze the whole truss as a rigid body (global ΣFx, ΣFy, ΣM) to find the support reactions first. You can't do joints yet because every joint touching a support has too many unknowns until the reaction is known.

      Phase 2 — Joints. Click a joint on the diagram. If it still has more than two unknown member forces, the game will tell you to look elsewhere — exactly like a sudoku cell with too many candidates. Find a joint with ≤2 unknowns, apply ΣFx=0 / ΣFy=0 at that joint by hand, and type in the member forces.

      Members: positive = tension (pulling), negative = compression (pushing).
      Reactions: positive = the arrow direction drawn on the diagram.

      Solved members turn green when in tension, pink when in compression. A true zero is a perfectly valid (and common!) answer — those are called zero-force members.

      Stuck? Hit the 💡 Hint button — it'll glow a joint you're actually allowed to solve next, without doing the math for you.
